# Information Design and Spatial Utilization

A structurally original layout can still feel empty, cramped, or under-designed.

Use this after Structural Direction and before final polish.

Read `presentation-rhythm.md` for typography-density coordination.

## Every large region needs a job

Ask:

- What decision does this area support?
- What relationship becomes easier to see?
- What changes over time?
- What context belongs near the primary object?
- Is empty space creating focus or exposing an incomplete information model?

If a region has no job, compress it, remove it, give it native context, or change the structure.

## Quiet versus empty

Quiet:

- clear hierarchy
- controlled density
- intentional breathing room
- important relationships remain visible

Empty:

- repeated blank columns
- fixed-height areas with little content
- oversized hero/work regions
- decorative gaps with no grouping purpose

## Dense versus compressed

Dense:

- useful simultaneous information
- clear grouping
- comfortable scanning
- readable roles
- strong alignment

Compressed:

- microtype doing most of the work
- repeated labels consuming space
- controls packed tighter than their usage frequency warrants
- too many items competing at once
- mobile preserving desktop density by shrinking

Do not treat density as a virtue by itself.

## Use domain-native context, not filler

Operations may need queue depth, age/SLA pressure, throughput/capacity, handoffs, and exceptions attached to stages.

Research may need comparison context, evidence, movement/rank, selected-object relationships, notes, and provenance.

Travel may need route/travel time, reservation constraints, geographic grouping, and opening time.

Store/fandom may need collection relationship, character/series context, preorder/release state, related items, and favorites continuity.

Use the context that helps the task. Do not add filler metrics because space exists.

## Information compression order

When more working information must fit, prefer:

1. remove repetition
2. share context
3. group related information
4. align comparable values
5. use a better representation
6. defer genuinely secondary detail
7. tighten spacing where the relationship supports it
8. only then reduce type size

Do not buy density primarily with smaller text.

## Density follows region and usage

Long-session tools can support compact regions when scanning remains comfortable.

Reading/evidence regions often need more breathing room than index or comparison regions.

Browse/consumer surfaces may alternate dense discovery with quieter focus.

Do not apply one density level everywhere.

## Spatial utilization test

Desktop:

- Is width used intentionally?
- Are wide columns carrying enough information?
- Are margins/padding consuming the product?
- Is a region empty because focus needs it, or because information design is incomplete?
- Is density achieved by structure or by microtype?

Mobile:

- Did information priority transform?
- Did the primary object survive?
- Did secondary context transform rather than disappear randomly?
- Did the page become an endless stack?
- Did the design shrink text instead of changing composition?
