# Concept Proof

Complete this proof before starting the full build of a substantial new or replacement surface.

Build a runnable vertical slice that a reviewer can operate.

## What to prove

Choose the smallest slice that exposes the main risks:

- primary object or task
- primary visual material
- highest-risk interaction
- one state change
- entry and return
- one second action
- wide and narrow representation
- pointer, touch, and keyboard implications
- loading or transition behavior when concept-critical

Use real or representative content, including at least one difficult record.

## Fidelity

Use the fidelity needed to judge the decision.

Low-style sketches are useful for structure, but they cannot prove:

- image or illustration quality
- typography character
- motion and response
- crop behavior
- optical hierarchy
- platform feel
- whether a premium direction survives implementation

The primary visual material and any claimed signature interaction must be near enough to final quality that the team can reject them honestly.

## Signature experience

Use a conventional solution when it serves the shaped problem. A signature experience is optional. When claiming one, record the domain truth it expresses, the user capability it improves, why a conventional treatment is insufficient, and what distinct observable behavior the reviewer operated. The benefit might be understanding, manipulation, comparison, exploration, decision-making, continuity, perceiving material or state, or repeated-use efficiency.

A renamed or custom-coded gallery, modal, carousel, tab switch, crop toggle, or decorative animation does not qualify on its own. If the claimed benefit is material to the concept and the slice does not demonstrate it, revise or reject before SELECT. A conventional pattern can remain useful once the unsupported claim is dropped.

## Alternatives

When the solution is open, compare two or three materially different models.

They should differ in:

- spatial model
- interaction model
- content priority
- visual material strategy
- signature opportunity

Do not spend the budget making several complete polished sites.

## Proof sequence

Operate:

1. arrive
2. identify the primary object or task
3. act
4. observe local and global response
5. continue to a second action
6. return or reverse
7. repeat at narrow width
8. use keyboard or touch-equivalent input where applicable

Link the runnable artifact in the concept-proof record. Resolve the calibration risk with an observed result before SELECT. Record operated states, what remains anchored, what changes, whether special continuity is warranted, and any missing proof. Apply the solution decision in `review-stages.md` before advancing.

## Pass criteria

The proof passes when:

- product principles are visible without explanation
- primary content shapes the composition
- response starts at the point of action
- spatial and conceptual continuity remain legible
- visual material supports the intended feeling at required scale
- familiar foundations do not fight the signature experience
- mobile has a deliberate interaction model
- likely performance and implementation cost are acceptable
- no concept-level issue is being deferred as “polish”

## Reject or reshape

Reject or reshape when:

- the concept depends on filler copy
- the signature is only a visual effect
- the primary action lives in detached chrome without product reason
- interaction becomes a generic modal, card, or page swap despite a stronger object relationship
- material quality cannot reach the proposed direction
- mobile requires preserving an impossible desktop composition
- repeated use would become ceremonial or slow
- the best argument for the concept is novelty

Discarding a concept is progress. Do not protect sunk implementation cost.
