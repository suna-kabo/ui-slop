# Review Stages

Use separate reviews for problem, solution, and implementation.

Mixing them produces pixel critique of the wrong product.

## Problem review

Review the Experience Brief, not mockups.

Outcomes:

- **PASS** — the user need, outcome, scope, and constraints are clear
- **RESHAPE** — the problem or scope needs change
- **BLOCKED** — material evidence or authority is missing

Questions:

- Is the user need recognizable and worth solving?
- Is the outcome end-to-end rather than screen-level?
- Is the irreducible core small enough to execute well?
- Are non-goals protecting focus?
- Should the product do less or use an existing convention?
- Are we solving a user problem or decorating a requested UI?

Record the finding and decision. RESHAPE returns to the brief. BLOCKED pauses concept commitment.

## Solution review

Review the concept proof.

Outcomes:

- **SELECT** — concept is strong enough to build
- **REVISE** — responsible layer can be improved without changing the model
- **REJECT** — structure, interaction model, material strategy, or platform fit is wrong
- **BLOCKED** — proof fidelity or required state is insufficient

Questions:

- Are product principles visible in structure and behavior?
- Does the primary content receive the best space?
- Is custom work concentrated where it matters?
- Is the signature experience specific and useful?
- Is interaction more understandable in use than in a screenshot?
- Does detail preserve origin and return?
- Is the visual material credible at required scale?
- Does narrow width change the model intentionally?
- Is the concept worth its technical and performance cost?

SELECT permits full build. REVISE requires another proof. REJECT returns to shaping or calibration before proving another concept. BLOCKED pauses the build.

## Implementation review

Review the built product on intended platforms and inputs.

Outcomes:

- **READY**
- **NOT_READY**
- **BLOCKED**

Record what you operated and what the evidence showed. NOT_READY requires correction and re-review. BLOCKED requires obtaining the missing evidence.

Inspect:

- fidelity to selected concept
- typography and copy
- image, illustration, icon, 3D, or data-viz quality
- alignment, rhythm, crop, density, and optical details
- local response to hover, focus, press, touch, selection, drag, save, and filter
- continuity across open, close, Back, forward, and responsive transformation
- loading, empty, error, retry, success, persistence, and recovery
- keyboard, focus, accessibility, and reduced motion
- runtime, layout shift, input latency, dropped frames, and platform fit
- weakest visible regions

## First-impression review

At solution and final implementation stages, give a reviewer the real viewport with no generator explanation.

Allow a brief inspection, then ask:

- What feels obviously wrong?
- What looks unnecessary?
- What looks cheap, generic, awkward, or unfinished?
- What receives attention without earning it?
- What would stop you trusting or wanting to continue?
- Where does the product feel alive or dead?

A first impression is not the whole review, but material issues found immediately are high-priority evidence.

## Independent review

For substantial work, prefer a reviewer who did not generate the concept.

Give them:

1. original brief and product principles
2. runnable artifact or immutable evidence
3. no victory narrative
4. generator explanation only after unprompted findings

A reviewer must explain a quality veto in product terms. “I do not like it” is insufficient.
