# Visual Commitment

A strong visual direction can still feel timid if it appears only in accents while the rest of the surface stays generic.

Choose how much of the **whole surface** belongs to the visual world.

## Commitment levels

These are not themes. They describe how much of the page participates.

### Quiet instrument

Best when repeated task completion dominates.

Identity lives in:

- structure
- typography
- spacing
- selected/focus states
- precise dividers
- a small number of material/color moments

Most of the surface remains calm.

Use when expression must not slow scanning.

### Anchored world

A substantial part of the surface carries the product identity.

Examples:

- one structural field owns a strong material/color
- collection navigation changes the surrounding environment
- the selected object changes a meaningful region
- a research/creative surface has a recognizable page-scale material language

The rest supports rather than competes.

### Full world

The visual world is inseparable from the experience.

Appropriate for some:

- fandom
- games
- media
- portfolios
- campaigns
- cultural products
- immersive showcases

Color, imagery, typography, geometry, and motion may all participate at page scale.

The interface still needs clear hierarchy and accessible controls.

## Choose before picking accents

Do not start with:

> neutral shell + one accent

unless Quiet instrument is actually the right commitment.

Ask:

- Does the product benefit from being visually memorable?
- How often is the surface used?
- How high are the consequences of mistakes?
- Is browsing/identity part of the value?
- Does the subject already have strong visual material?
- Would page-scale expression improve or obstruct the task?

## Surface-mode tendency

Not a rule, but a useful prior:

- Workflow: Quiet instrument or Anchored world
- Reading: Quiet instrument or Anchored world
- Persuasion: Anchored world or Full world
- Immersion: often Full world
- Browse / Collect: Anchored world or Full world

Override this when the product truth says otherwise.

## Commit consistently

Once a commitment level is chosen, secondary regions must support it.

Do not create:

- a highly authored campaign area
- followed by a generic admin filter panel
- followed by a default modal
- followed by an unrelated mobile sheet

The visual world should survive into utilities without making them noisy.

## Commitment test

Zoom out.

Ask:

- Is the chosen world visible at page scale?
- Is identity only a logo/accent?
- Do secondary surfaces suddenly look like another product?
- Is the interface timid because everything meaningful was pushed into one hero?

If the surface claims identity but 80% of the page is generic neutral UI, the commitment is probably too weak.
