# UI Slop repeatability validation

**Release recommendation — `KEEP_EXPERIMENTAL`.** B delivered repeatable local strengths in the Operations second run and fit recovery in Ceramics, while the full blinded sample did not show better first-pass design or stronger Finals across product families. A was preferred in seven of twelve Initial judgments and nine of twelve Final judgments. B was preferred in the remaining five and three. These are counts of paired choices by two reviewers per pair, with only six distinct experimental pairs.

The study ran three practical briefs twice under each frozen skill. It compares first runnable implementations with their reviewed revisions and retains the applications, browser evidence, and anonymized observations. The [original Kilnroom diagnosis](HISTORICAL.md) and [reproduction guide](REPRODUCE.md) cover the authentic artifacts and executable commands.

## First-pass design and correction effectiveness

Initial includes choices already made and tested during the concept proof. Final includes the full-product self-review and correction round. Each run retained a runnable proof, and write events show no Initial mutation after Final work began. Browser tests and notes establish where a particular defect changed. They offer limited evidence about why the model made a choice. All twelve used the same time limit. Successful A sessions had median elapsed time 466 seconds and 17.5 browser tool calls. B sessions had 457 seconds and 15.5 calls. Median output usage was about 19.3k tokens for A and 19.4k for B. Those medians exclude the infrastructure timeout and the preflight sessions. Input-token counts include repeated context and cache hits. The data provide no indication that B's corrections came from a consistently larger review budget. Calls include probe, retries and regression, so counting them cannot establish detection quality.

- **Ceramics.** Three of the four mobile openings include a four-day occupancy axis. All have named loads and meaningful shelf area, but the initial scheduling and fit details vary. B's `86cf04199e` retained a readable phone axis and turned a failed fit at Ash into a direct link to compatible MC-16. A's `0eb8ac9098` exposed the manifest without a selection and retained load continuity across roles. Its initial 768px timeline clipped part of necessary occupancy information and a very short added window needed a readable alternative. Final adds a separate entry for the short window and raises selected detail. In Final `86cf04199e`, the rapid lead switch and reopen can still show member actions over the new lead board. The source and temporal trace tie this to the role update completing after the already-open modal rendered. A predictable close/reopen recovers, but the wrong role's actions block the immediate staff continuation. See `reviewers/3-2/process/3-1440-role-continuity/trace.zip` and `3-390-progress-confirm/trace.zip` in the same reviewer folder.
- **Collection.** All four versions show locally authored location-specific illustrations. The B entries choose larger first-pass introductions. B run 1 had the clearly visible “yournext” word join and revealed less actionable content at launch. Its revision repaired the word join, shortened the crop and made shortlist return more helpful after removing the current entry. A run 1 still exposed terrain and tide constraints sooner. A run 2 learned to resume filters and sort after a detail reload. B run 2 already did this in its Initial. Both run-2 reviewers preferred its initial continuity, then preferred A's improved Final composition and visible trip facts. In B run 1 an independent interrupted-detail test still returned to all eight places instead of the filtered decision set. See `reviewers/4-2/process/3-390-detail-reload/trace.zip` and `reviewers/1-1/process/initial-4a-mobile-keyboard/9.jpg` for different return paths.
- **Operations.** A run 1 exposed turbidity and conductivity earlier and offered an immediate independent comparison route. B run 1 gave identifiers and lab evidence stronger hierarchy and kept save, exclusion, Undo and next match together. Its Initial often revealed the top of a comparison without its readings. Final scrolls to the actual aligned comparison and preserves a draft through mobile reload. One reviewer preferred its completed handoff, the other still preferred A's short mobile route and easier sample substitution. B run 2 won both Initial and Final reviews for its readable register and clear actions while continuing to the next matched sample. Both baselines made meaningful corrections, including Back recovery and focus. B run 2 could show a red retry validation alongside a completed Accepted state during a repeated submission, without duplicating the mutation. The first reviewer also found unstable focus return after reloading a filtered compare. See `reviewers/5-1/process/final-a08e70c127-390-cross/14.jpg` and `reviewers/6-2/process/4-390-draft/6.jpg`.

Typography and composition converged across the sample. All twelve use large serif display type on their primary opening, and most use restrained greens, paper surfaces and small colored status treatments. The collection apps share an illustrated masthead, quiet cards and image-overlay saves. The operational tools share a register/detail division and dense badges, including one genuinely dark baseline. The ceramics apps use program bands and either a visible ledger, modal or fixed workspace. Some conventions fit the work. The rendered evidence points to common defaults in both conditions and supplies no basis for attributing the shared fingerprint to B alone. There is no repeated gain in status hierarchy, removal of surplus introduction or perceived smoothness across all three families. Some outcomes improved through immediate local feedback or revealing the next useful control without adding motion. There is no field latency or physical-device comparison. ## Narrow follow-up to the skill

The historical `v3-test` early-start result and the preceding investigation’s candidate cooling carryover share a mechanism. A reviewer can follow a valid local transition while a different affected surface projects an old fact. The rapid role mismatch in `86cf04199e` provides a fresh example. The follow-up adds two sentences to the existing Browser QA primary-sequence guidance. After a consequential change that users consume in more than one view, follow the same record and verify the changed fact there. If views intentionally report different facts, check that users can distinguish what the values mean at the point of action.

The fresh held-out [equipment checkout challenge](targeted/README.md) has one valid and one divergent projection. Both cases confirmed that camera kit EQ-18 was issued at 10:20 with a 12:00 reserved pickup. At 1440, 390 and 320px, confirmation-only assertions passed in both cases. The companion inventory reported Available at desk after issue only in case 62. Case 75 reported Unavailable · issued. Both persisted across reload, responded immediately, and returned correctly to Available after reversal. The paired cases show why looking at both projections matters and protect legitimate planned/actual labels. This post-experiment check establishes coverage of a specific failure mechanism. It leaves any effect on general reviewer detection or initial design quality open.

A separate reviewer reproduced the discrepancy at 1365 × 860 and 390 × 844, confirmed visible keyboard focus and reload, and inspected source after opening and operating both cases. The reviewer recorded the false availability as a quality veto and marked the tested local behavior verified. This report classifies the same observed discrepancy as an Evidence Gate failure because accurate inventory availability is an explicit test contract. The observation is shared and the classification difference is retained. See `targeted-retests/independent/report.md` and `targeted-retests/deterministic/results.json`.
