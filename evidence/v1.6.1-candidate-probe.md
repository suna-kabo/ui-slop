# v1.6.1 candidate browser probe

This probe records the concrete continuity failures that motivated the v1.6.1 candidate and the later v1.7 Task Continuity model.

## Parcel Relay

The analyst selected 30 days and Coast, then moved between hubs and inspected details.

Shared state behaved correctly: comparison and trend context reacted to the selected hub, and detail could be opened without losing the chosen hub.

The material friction appeared on repeated mobile inspection:

- selecting a low ledger row left the only Inspect action above the viewport
- the analyst had to scroll away from the selected row
- opening and closing detail restored focus near the remote Inspect control
- the selected row and neighboring hubs were then far below the viewport

The first action worked. The repeated task did not preserve locality.

Reload also preserved the selected hub while resetting date and region, changing the meaning of the comparison without restoring the previous filter context.

## SYNC/RUSH

The shopper entered a collection, filtered to a character and item type, opened a product, favorited it, and followed related merchandise.

Single-detail return preserved the original filtered rack and focus.

Two continuity problems remained:

- entering and leaving Saved could return to an unfiltered catalog rather than the previous browse context
- following product A to related product B replaced the detail context so Back returned directly to the rack instead of product A

Archive/collection transitions also changed browse framing in ways driven more by implementation than an explicit return contract.

## Conclusion

The failures were not primarily visual.

They showed that reactive shared state and context-preserving detail still need explicit rules for:

- where the next likely action lives
- which state survives navigation
- how nested detail uses history
- what Back/close restores
- how browse modes relate
- how repeated work behaves on narrow screens

The v1.6.1 candidate added second-pass QA and relationship placement guidance. v1.7 generalizes that evidence into Task Continuity.
